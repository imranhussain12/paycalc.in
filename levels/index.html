8th CPC Salary by Pay Level: All 19 Levels

Updated September 2026 Levels 1 to 18, including 13A · projections, allowances and pension

Every Central Government post sits in one cell of the pay matrix — a single table with 19 levels across the top and up to 40 annual increment stages down each column. Your level decides not just basic pay but your transport allowance band, your CGHS contribution, and ultimately your pension.

This page covers all of them: what each level pays now, what it is projected to pay under the 8th Pay Commission, who works there, and how you move up.

Every pay level at a glance

Entry pay is the first cell in each column; the maximum is the top of the column after roughly 40 years of increments. Projections apply the fitment factor to entry pay — 2.28 is where independent estimates currently cluster, 2.57 repeats the 7th CPC number, and 2.86 is the figure employee federations have demanded.

What each level actually means

Open any level for the cadres it covers, the recruitment route into it, the promotion path out of it, and the allowances, deductions and pension that apply.

Level 1 — Grade Pay 1800 · ₹18,000 to ₹56,900

Who is here: Multi-Tasking Staff (MTS), safaiwalas, chowkidars, daftaries, gardeners and peons across every central ministry, plus Group D equivalents in the Railways and India Post.

How you get in: SSC Multi-Tasking Staff examination, open to candidates who have passed Class 10. It is the single largest entry point into central government service.

Moving up: Promotion to Level 2 (Lower Division Clerk) comes through a departmental seniority quota or a limited departmental competitive examination, typically after 3–8 years depending on the ministry’s vacancy position.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Levels 1–2 band (₹1,350 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹900 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹250 a month; CGEGIS ₹30 as Group C. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹2,880 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹4,032.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹9,000 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹28,450 at the top of the column. Because pension is 50% of last basic drawn, an MTS retiring near the top of Level 1 secures a far better pension than one who stagnates mid-column, which is why MACP upgradations matter enormously at this level.

Worth knowing: Level 1 has the longest column in the matrix — 40 stages — so an MTS recruited at 20 can spend an entire career inside the level and still reach ₹56,900 basic through annual increments alone.

Level 2 — Grade Pay 1900 · ₹19,900 to ₹63,200

Who is here: Lower Division Clerks (LDC) in central secretariat and subordinate offices, Postal Assistants and Sorting Assistants in India Post, and equivalent clerical grades in the Railways.

How you get in: SSC Combined Higher Secondary Level (CHSL) examination for Class 12 passouts, and by promotion from Level 1.

Moving up: Promotion to Level 4 (Upper Division Clerk) usually follows after 5–8 years, either on seniority or through a departmental examination.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Levels 1–2 band (₹1,350 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹900 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹250 a month; CGEGIS ₹30 as Group C. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹3,184 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹4,458.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹9,950 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹31,600 at the top of the column. A clerk who enters at Level 2 and reaches Level 6 or 7 by retirement typically triples their pension compared with stagnating in the entry grade.

Worth knowing: Level 2 is a pass-through grade for most: very few clerks retire here, since both the seniority quota and MACP move them upward well before superannuation.

Level 3 — Grade Pay 2000 · ₹21,700 to ₹69,100

Who is here: Constables in the CAPFs (CRPF, BSF, CISF, ITBP, SSB) and Delhi Police, skilled tradesmen and technicians in the Railways and defence establishments.

How you get in: SSC GD Constable examination and force-specific recruitment drives, plus Railway RRB Group D and technician examinations.

Moving up: Constables move to Head Constable (Level 4) on seniority and departmental tests; technicians progress through the skilled and highly-skilled grades.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Levels 3–8 band (₹3,600 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹1,800 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹250 a month; CGEGIS ₹30 as Group C. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹3,472 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹4,861.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹10,850 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹34,550 at the top of the column. CAPF personnel recruited on or after 1 January 2004 are on NPS; the long-running demand for restoration of the old pension scheme for paramilitary forces is one of the live issues before the 8th CPC.

Worth knowing: Uniformed personnel at this level draw additional risk and hardship allowances that civilian staff do not, so the payslip often exceeds what the matrix alone suggests.

Level 4 — Grade Pay 2400 · ₹25,500 to ₹81,100

Who is here: Upper Division Clerks, Stenographer Grade D, Head Constables in the CAPFs and Delhi Police, and Junior Clerks in the Railways.

How you get in: SSC CHSL for stenographer posts and SSC CGL for some UDC-equivalent posts, plus promotion from Levels 2 and 3.

Moving up: Onward movement is to Level 5 and then Level 6, where the step from Grade Pay 2800 to 4200 is the single biggest percentage jump in the Group C ladder.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Levels 3–8 band (₹3,600 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹1,800 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹250 a month; CGEGIS ₹30 as Group C. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹4,080 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹5,712.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹12,750 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹40,550 at the top of the column. Reaching Level 6 before retirement roughly doubles the pension of an employee who retires from Level 4, which is why the Grade Pay 4200 barrier dominates cadre-restructuring demands.

Worth knowing: Level 4 sits just below the Grade Pay 2800 ceiling, where a large number of clerical staff spend their middle career years.

Level 5 — Grade Pay 2800 · ₹29,200 to ₹92,300

Who is here: Senior clerks, Assistant Sub-Inspectors in the CAPFs and Delhi Police, senior technical staff and laboratory assistants.

How you get in: Predominantly by promotion from Level 4, with limited direct recruitment through SSC CGL for certain technical posts.

Moving up: The next step, Level 6, is the gateway into the supervisory grades and carries a substantially higher transport allowance band.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Levels 3–8 band (₹3,600 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹1,800 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹250 a month; CGEGIS ₹30 as Group C. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹4,672 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹6,541.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹14,600 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹46,150 at the top of the column. Employees who receive their second MACP upgradation at 20 years typically land at or just above Level 5.

Worth knowing: Level 5 is the top of the Grade Pay 2800 cluster and the last level before the transport allowance band changes at Level 6.

Level 6 — Grade Pay 4200 · ₹35,400 to ₹1,12,400

Who is here: Inspectors in several departments, Junior Engineers in CPWD, the Railways and MES, Sub-Inspectors in the CAPFs and Delhi Police, senior secondary teachers in KVS and NVS, and Assistants in subordinate offices.

How you get in: SSC Combined Graduate Level (CGL) for graduates, SSC Junior Engineer for diploma and degree holders, and KVS/NVS recruitment for teaching posts.

Moving up: Level 6 leads to Level 7, and for engineers onward to the Assistant Executive Engineer grades. It is also the level most commonly reached by Group C staff through MACP.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Levels 3–8 band (₹3,600 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹1,800 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹450 a month; CGEGIS ₹60 as Group B. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹5,664 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹7,930.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹17,700 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹56,200 at the top of the column. Level 6 is the effective floor for a comfortable central government pension: at the top of the column basic pay reaches ₹1,12,400, giving a pension of ₹56,200 before Dearness Relief.

Worth knowing: Crossing into Level 6 raises transport allowance from the ₹1,350/₹900 band to ₹3,600/₹1,800 and increases the CGHS contribution from ₹250 to ₹450 — the first level at which both change.

Level 7 — Grade Pay 4600 · ₹44,900 to ₹1,42,400

Who is here: Assistant Section Officers in the Central Secretariat Service, Inspectors in CBIC and CBDT, Superintendents, Assistant Enforcement Officers, and Sub-Divisional Officers in engineering cadres.

How you get in: SSC CGL is the dominant route — Inspector (Income Tax / Central Excise) and Assistant Section Officer are among the most sought-after results of that examination.

Moving up: Level 7 feeds into Level 8, and in the CSS to Section Officer at Level 8, then Under Secretary at Level 11.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Levels 3–8 band (₹3,600 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹1,800 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹650 a month; CGEGIS ₹60 as Group B. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹7,184 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹10,058.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹22,450 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹71,200 at the top of the column. An officer entering at Level 7 in their mid-twenties and progressing normally through Levels 8, 9 and 11 typically retires around Level 12, with a pension well above ₹1 lakh a month at current rates.

Worth knowing: Level 7 is where the CGHS contribution rises to ₹650 a month, and where most graduates entering through SSC CGL begin their careers.

Level 8 — Grade Pay 4800 · ₹47,600 to ₹1,51,100

Who is here: Section Officers in the Central Secretariat, Assistant Audit Officers and Assistant Accounts Officers in the Indian Audit and Accounts Department, and Income Tax Officers.

How you get in: Mainly promotion from Level 7, with a limited departmental competitive examination route. Assistant Audit Officer is filled directly through SSC CGL.

Moving up: Level 8 typically leads to Level 9, or directly to Level 10 in departments that operate a non-functional upgradation.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Levels 3–8 band (₹3,600 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹1,800 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹650 a month; CGEGIS ₹60 as Group B. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹7,616 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹10,662.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹23,800 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹75,550 at the top of the column. Assistant Audit Officers recruited directly at this level enjoy an unusually long runway, often retiring at Level 12 or above.

Worth knowing: Level 8 is the highest Group B gazetted grade in many organisations and the point at which officers begin exercising independent statutory powers.

Level 9 — Grade Pay 5400 (PB-2) · ₹53,100 to ₹1,67,800

Who is here: Deputy Superintendents, Section Officers after four years of service, Assistant Accounts Officers on upgradation, and the DANICS/DANIPS entry grades.

How you get in: Almost entirely by promotion. Grade Pay 5400 in Pay Band 2 is a long-standing anomaly, since the same grade pay in Pay Band 3 sits at Level 10.

Moving up: Movement to Level 10 or Level 11 follows, and this is the level at which transport allowance moves into the highest slab.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹650 a month; CGEGIS ₹60 as Group B. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹8,496 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹11,894.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹26,550 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹83,900 at the top of the column. The Level 9 versus Level 10 Grade Pay 5400 anomaly has direct pension consequences and is among the specific cadre issues placed before the 8th CPC.

Worth knowing: Level 9 is the first level in the ₹7,200/₹3,600 transport allowance band, a meaningful monthly difference once DA is applied on top.

Level 10 — Grade Pay 5400 (PB-3) · ₹56,100 to ₹1,77,500

Who is here: The Group A entry grade — IAS, IPS, IFS and IRS probationers, Assistant Commissioners, Assistant Directors and equivalent officers across the organised Group A services.

How you get in: UPSC Civil Services Examination, UPSC Engineering Services Examination, Combined Medical Services and the Indian Forest Service examination.

Moving up: Officers move to Senior Time Scale (Level 11) after roughly four years, then Junior Administrative Grade (Level 12) at about nine years.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹650 a month; CGEGIS ₹120 as Group A. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹8,976 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹12,566.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹28,050 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹88,750 at the top of the column. A direct-recruit officer entering Level 10 at 25 and progressing on schedule can reach Level 14 or 15 by retirement, placing the pension in the ₹90,000–₹1,10,000 range at current basic rates.

Worth knowing: Level 10 carries the same Grade Pay 5400 as Level 9 but in Pay Band 3, which is why direct-recruit Group A officers start ahead of promotees on the same nominal grade pay.

Level 11 — Grade Pay 6600 · ₹67,700 to ₹2,08,700

Who is here: Senior Time Scale officers — Under Secretaries in central ministries, Deputy Commissioners, Additional Superintendents of Police and equivalent ranks.

How you get in: By promotion after approximately four years of Group A service, and by promotion from Level 9 or 10 in subordinate services.

Moving up: Next is Level 12, the Junior Administrative Grade, usually at around nine years of service.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹650 a month; CGEGIS ₹120 as Group A. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹10,832 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹15,165.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹33,850 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹1,04,350 at the top of the column. The CGHS contribution stays at ₹650 through Level 11 and rises to ₹1,000 from Level 12 onward.

Worth knowing: Level 11 is where officers first take independent charge of a division or district-level office in most services.

Level 12 — Grade Pay 7600 · ₹78,800 to ₹2,09,200

Who is here: Junior Administrative Grade — Deputy Secretaries in central ministries, Joint Commissioners, and Superintendents of Police in larger districts.

How you get in: Promotion at roughly nine years of Group A service, subject to empanelment and vacancy.

Moving up: Level 13, the Selection Grade or Director level, follows at about 13–14 years.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹1,000 a month; CGEGIS ₹120 as Group A. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹12,608 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹17,651.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹39,400 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹1,04,600 at the top of the column. Level 12 is the point from which non-functional upgradation rules begin to matter for officers of organised Group A services relative to their IAS batchmates.

Worth knowing: From Level 12 the CGHS contribution rises to ₹1,000 a month and CGEGIS moves to the Group A rate.

Level 13 — Grade Pay 8700 · ₹1,23,100 to ₹2,15,900

Who is here: Selection Grade — Directors in central ministries, Conservators of Forests, Deputy Inspectors General in some services, and Commissioners in revenue departments.

How you get in: Promotion at approximately 13–14 years of Group A service, with central empanelment required for ministry postings.

Moving up: Level 13A and Level 14 follow, the latter being the Joint Secretary grade.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹1,000 a month; CGEGIS ₹120 as Group A. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹19,696 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹27,574.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹61,550 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹1,07,950 at the top of the column. Reaching Level 13 effectively guarantees a pension above ₹60,000 in current basic terms before Dearness Relief.

Worth knowing: The jump from Level 12 to Level 13 is the largest single step in the matrix — ₹78,800 to ₹1,23,100 — because the 7th CPC applied a 2.67 multiple here rather than 2.57, correcting a Grade Pay 8700 anomaly.

Level 14 — Grade Pay 10000 · ₹1,44,200 to ₹2,18,200

Who is here: Joint Secretaries to the Government of India, Inspectors General of Police, Chief Engineers and Commissioners.

How you get in: Promotion at roughly 18–20 years of service, with central empanelment required for Joint Secretary postings.

Moving up: Level 15, the Additional Secretary grade in the Higher Administrative Grade, comes next.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹1,000 a month; CGEGIS ₹120 as Group A. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹23,072 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹32,301.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹72,100 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹1,09,100 at the top of the column. Officers retiring from Level 14 draw a pension of roughly ₹72,000–₹1,09,000 in current basic terms, depending on their stage in the column.

Worth knowing: Joint Secretary is the first level at which an officer routinely signs decisions on behalf of the Government of India.

Level 15 — Higher Administrative Grade · ₹1,82,200 to ₹2,24,100

Who is here: Additional Secretaries to the Government of India, Additional Directors General of Police, and heads of major attached offices.

How you get in: Promotion at roughly 25–30 years of service, subject to empanelment.

Moving up: Level 16 (HAG+) is next, followed by the apex scales.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹1,000 a month; CGEGIS ₹120 as Group A. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹29,152 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹40,813.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹91,100 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹1,12,050 at the top of the column. From Level 15 upward pension approaches the ceiling, since apex pay itself is capped at ₹2,50,000.

Worth knowing: The Higher Administrative Grade compresses sharply — the whole of Level 15 spans only ₹1,82,200 to ₹2,24,100.

Level 16 — HAG+ · ₹2,05,400 to ₹2,24,400

Who is here: Special Secretaries, Directors General of major organisations, and Members of statutory boards.

How you get in: Promotion within the Higher Administrative Grade structure.

Moving up: Level 17, the apex scale, follows for the small number of officers who reach Secretary rank.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹1,000 a month; CGEGIS ₹120 as Group A. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹32,864 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹46,010.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹1,02,700 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹1,12,200 at the top of the column. Pension at this level is close to the structural maximum for central government service.

Worth knowing: Level 16 spans barely ₹19,000 from entry to maximum, the narrowest band in the matrix apart from the fixed apex levels.

Level 17 — Apex Scale (fixed) · ₹2,25,000 (fixed)

Who is here: Secretaries to the Government of India and Directors General of the CAPFs.

How you get in: Appointment by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet from empanelled Additional Secretaries.

Moving up: Only Level 18, the Cabinet Secretary, sits above.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹1,000 a month; CGEGIS ₹120 as Group A. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹36,000 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹50,400.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹1,12,500 retiring at entry stage. Pension is fixed at ₹1,12,500 in current basic terms, half of the apex pay.

Worth knowing: Level 17 is a fixed pay level — there are no annual increments, because the officer is already at apex scale.

Level 18 — Cabinet Secretary (fixed) · ₹2,50,000 (fixed)

Who is here: The Cabinet Secretary — the senior-most civil servant in the Government of India, and the only post at this level.

How you get in: Appointed by the Appointments Committee of the Cabinet, conventionally for a fixed tenure.

Moving up: There is no level above.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹1,000 a month; CGEGIS ₹120 as Group A. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹40,000 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹56,000.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹1,25,000 retiring at entry stage. Pension is ₹1,25,000 in current basic terms, the structural maximum for a central government pensioner.

Worth knowing: Level 18 is a single-post, fixed-pay level with no increments. The ratio between it and Level 1 — about 1:13.9 — is the compression ratio pay commissions use as a benchmark.

Level 13A — Grade Pay 8900 · ₹1,31,100 to ₹2,16,600

Who is here: Deputy Inspectors General in the CAPFs and state police cadres, and equivalent ranks in other uniformed services.

How you get in: Promotion from Level 13, largely within the police and paramilitary structure.

Moving up: Level 14, the Inspector General grade, is next.

Allowances: transport allowance in the Level 9 and above band (₹7,200 in higher-TPTA cities, ₹3,600 elsewhere, plus DA on that amount); CGHS ₹1,000 a month; CGEGIS ₹120 as Group A. NPS takes 10% of Basic + DA — ₹20,976 at entry pay — with the government adding ₹29,366.

Pension: 50% of last basic — about ₹65,550 retiring at entry stage, up to ₹1,08,300 at the top of the column. Because Level 13A is a narrow band, officers usually pass through it quickly, so it rarely determines the final pension.

Worth knowing: Level 13A exists specifically to accommodate the DIG rank, which had no clean equivalent when the 7th CPC collapsed the old pay bands into the matrix.

Moving up without a promotion

Where no promotion arises, the Modified Assured Career Progression scheme grants three financial upgradations — at 10, 20 and 30 years of continuous service — each moving you to the next level in the matrix. For employees in stagnant cadres this is worth more over a career than the fitment factor, which is why staff federations have asked the 8th CPC for five upgradations rather than three.

On promotion, pay is fixed under FR 22(I)(a)(1): one notional increment in your existing level, then placement at the first cell in the new level equal to or higher than that figure. The gain comes from the level change, not the increment.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find my pay level?

It is printed on your payslip as "Level" or "Pay in Pay Matrix". If it is not shown, use your pre-2016 grade pay: 1800 is Level 1, 2400 is Level 4, 4200 is Level 6, 4600 is Level 7, 5400 in PB-3 is Level 10, and so on — the full mapping is in the table above.

What is the difference between Level 9 and Level 10?

Both carry Grade Pay 5400, but Level 9 sits in Pay Band 2 and Level 10 in Pay Band 3. That is why a direct-recruit Group A officer starts at ₹56,100 while a promotee on the same nominal grade pay starts at ₹53,100. It is a long-standing anomaly and one of the specific cadre issues placed before the 8th CPC.

How much does one increment add?

Three per cent of your current basic pay, rounded up to the next ₹100, granted on 1 January or 1 July. Because each increment is calculated on the new figure, progression compounds — which is why the top of a column is roughly three times its entry value despite a "3% a year" rule.

Which level do most Group C employees reach?

Level 6 is the effective ceiling for many, usually via MACP rather than promotion. It matters because Level 6 is where the transport allowance band and the CGHS slab both change, and because the top of the Level 6 column reaches ₹1,12,400 — a pension of ₹56,200 before Dearness Relief.

Will the pay matrix survive the 8th Pay Commission?

Almost certainly. The matrix introduced in 2016 is widely regarded as the 7th CPC's most successful reform. What changes is the value in each cell, multiplied by the fitment factor and rounded. Watch for whether the Level 13 anomaly correction is retained — the 7th CPC applied 2.67 there rather than 2.57.
