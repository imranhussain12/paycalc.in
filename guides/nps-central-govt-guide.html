NPS for Central Government Employees

Updated August 2026 By the PayCalc.in team · Sources cited throughout

Every Central Government employee who joined on or after 1 January 2004 is covered by the National Pension System. Here is how the contributions, the new UPS option, the tax rules and the exit rules actually work.

Who is covered

The NPS replaced the old defined-benefit pension for civilian Central Government recruits from 1 January 2004 (armed forces personnel remain on the old scheme). It is regulated by PFRDA, with accounts held as a Permanent Retirement Account Number (PRAN) through the CRA system (pfrda.org.in).

Contributions: 10% + 14%

ContributorRateBase
Employee (mandatory)10%Basic pay + DA
Government14%Basic pay + DA (since 1 April 2019)

Example at Level 7 (basic ₹44,900, DA 60%): base = ₹71,840. Your deduction: ₹7,184 per month; the government adds ₹10,058. Total monthly accretion to your PRAN: ₹17,242. Model the deduction in the take-home pay calculator.

The Unified Pension Scheme (UPS) option

From 1 April 2025, central employees under NPS may opt for the UPS, which grafts an assurance onto the NPS architecture (DoPPW, PIB):

  • Assured pension: 50% of the average basic pay of the final 12 months, for 25+ years of service (proportionate for 10–25 years).
  • Minimum pension: ₹10,000 per month after at least 10 years.
  • Family pension: 60% of the employee's pension.
  • Indexation: pension increases with inflation, akin to Dearness Relief.
  • The government's contribution under UPS is higher (an estimated 18.5% including the pool contribution), while the employee stays at 10%.

The choice is one-time. Broadly: UPS suits those who value certainty and long careers; pure NPS can outperform for early joiners comfortable with market returns. How the 8th CPC treats UPS pay definitions is one of the most watched questions of the upcoming report — see the 8th CPC guide.

How the account actually works

Every subscriber gets a Permanent Retirement Account Number (PRAN), portable across departments and states for your whole career. Contributions flow into a Tier I account, which is the retirement account and cannot be freely withdrawn. A voluntary Tier II account works like an ordinary investment account with no lock-in and no tax benefit for most subscribers.

Money is managed by pension fund managers appointed by PFRDA. Central government subscribers were historically placed in a default scheme split across three public-sector fund managers in fixed proportions, and can now also choose from a wider set of schemes and life-cycle options, which vary the equity share by age. Charges are among the lowest of any regulated retirement product in India, which compounds meaningfully over a thirty-year career.

You can track your balance through the CRA portal or the NPS mobile app, and you should — an annual check that your employer's 14% is actually being credited is worth the five minutes. Delayed remittances by drawing and disbursing officers are a recurring complaint.

Partial withdrawal and exit rules

While in service: after three years you may withdraw up to 25% of your own contributions (not the government's, and not the returns) for specified purposes — higher education or marriage of children, purchase or construction of a first house, treatment of specified critical illnesses, or skill development. A maximum of three such withdrawals is permitted over a career, and they are tax-free.

At superannuation (60): you may withdraw up to 60% of the corpus as a tax-free lump sum, and at least 40% must be used to purchase an annuity, which pays a monthly pension taxed as income. If the total corpus is ₹5 lakh or less, the entire amount may be withdrawn.

Exit before 60: the rules invert — at least 80% must buy an annuity and only 20% may be taken as a lump sum, unless the corpus is ₹2.5 lakh or less.

On death in service: the entire accumulated corpus is payable to the nominee or legal heir. Keeping your nomination current is one of the most consequential five-minute tasks in the whole system.

UPS or NPS: how to think about the choice

The Unified Pension Scheme, available since 1 April 2025, is not a separate scheme so much as a guarantee layered on the NPS structure. The choice is one-time and irreversible, so it deserves more than a glance.

Pure NPS can outperform for subscribers who joined young, have a high equity allocation and a thirty-year horizon — but with no floor if markets disappoint. How the 8th CPC treats the pay definitions underlying the UPS assurance is one of the open questions in its report.

Frequently asked questions

What are the NPS contribution rates for government employees?

Employee: 10% of Basic + DA (mandatory). Government: 14% of Basic + DA (since April 2019). Both flow into your Tier I PRAN account.

What is the Unified Pension Scheme (UPS)?

An option under NPS, operational from 1 April 2025, that guarantees an assured pension of 50% of the average basic pay of the last 12 months for employees with 25+ years of service, with proportionate benefits for 10–25 years, a minimum ₹10,000 pension, and inflation indexation.

Is NPS tax-deductible?

Your own 10% qualifies under 80CCD(1) within the 80C limit plus ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B) — old regime only. The government’s 14% under 80CCD(2) is deductible in both regimes, including the new regime.

Can I withdraw NPS money before retirement?

Partial withdrawals up to 25% of your own contributions are allowed after 3 years for specified purposes (illness, education, marriage, house), at most 3 times. At exit before 60, 80% of the corpus must buy an annuity.

What happens to NPS at retirement?

At 60, you can withdraw up to 60% of the corpus tax-free as a lump sum; at least 40% must purchase an annuity that pays a monthly pension. Under UPS, the assured-pension formula applies instead.

Can I check whether my employer is depositing the 14%?

Yes, through the CRA portal or the NPS mobile app using your PRAN. It is worth checking annually — delayed remittance by drawing and disbursing officers is a recurring complaint, and errors are easier to correct early.

How much can I withdraw from NPS before retirement?

Up to 25% of your own contributions after three years, for specified purposes such as children's education or marriage, buying a first house, critical illness or skill development. A maximum of three withdrawals over a career, and they are tax-free.

What happens to my NPS if I die in service?

The entire accumulated corpus is payable to your nominee or legal heir. Keeping your nomination up to date is essential.

Should I choose UPS or stay with NPS?

UPS gives an assured 50% of your last twelve months' average basic pay after 25 years, with inflation indexation and a ₹10,000 minimum — better for those who value certainty and will complete long service. Pure NPS keeps the market upside but offers no floor. The choice is one-time and irreversible.

Is the government's NPS contribution taxable?

No. The government's contribution is deductible under Section 80CCD(2) in both the old and the new tax regimes, up to 14% of Basic + DA for government employees.
