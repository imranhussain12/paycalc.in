Dearness Allowance (महंगाई भत्ता): The Complete Guide

Updated August 2026 By the PayCalc.in team · Sources cited throughout

DA is the inflation shield of every government salary and pension. Here is how the 60% rate is computed, the full history since 2016, and what happens when the 8th CPC merges it away.

How DA is calculated

DA is linked to the All-India Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (AICPI-IW), compiled monthly by the Labour Bureau, Shimla (labourbureau.gov.in). The formula for the 7th CPC period:

DA % = [(12-month avg. of AICPI-IW − 261.42) ÷ 261.42] × 100

261.42 is the linking value of the index on 1 January 2016 (converted to the 2016=100 base series), the date DA was reset to zero.

The rate is revised effective 1 January and 1 July each year. Because the Cabinet announcement usually comes two to three months later (around Holi and Diwali, as it happens), the intervening difference is paid as DA arrears.

Working out a DA revision yourself

The formula uses a twelve-month average, not a single month's index, which is why a spike in one month barely moves the rate.

DA % = [(average AICPI-IW over 12 months − 261.42) ÷ 261.42] × 100

For a revision effective 1 January, the twelve months are January to December of the preceding year. For 1 July, they are July to June. The Labour Bureau publishes the index around the last working day of each month with a two-month lag, so the twelve-month window for a January revision is only complete at the end of February — which is why announcements arrive in March or later.

The result is rounded to the nearest whole number, and the Department of Expenditure issues the office memorandum that makes it payable. Between the effective date and the announcement, employees continue on the old rate and receive the difference as arrears.

What DA is paid on — and what it is not

DA is a percentage of basic pay only. It is not paid on HRA, and not on gross salary. One important exception: Transport Allowance attracts DA on itself, so a Level 9 employee drawing ₹7,200 TPTA actually receives ₹11,520 at 60% DA. This is easy to miss when reconciling a payslip.

DA also feeds several other calculations. NPS contributions are 10% of Basic + DA (with the government adding 14% on the same base), so every DA rise increases your retirement corpus. Overtime allowance and some compensatory allowances are computed on Basic + DA. And gratuity on retirement uses Basic + DA as the base, so the DA rate on your last working day has a lasting effect.

CDA, IDA and bank DA are different systems

People often compare rates that are not comparable.

  • CDA (Central Dearness Allowance) — the system described on this page, revised half-yearly for central government employees and pensioners.
  • IDA (Industrial Dearness Allowance) — used by central public sector undertakings, revised quarterly and linked to the same index but on a different base and formula. IDA percentages are not comparable with CDA percentages.
  • Bank DA — governed by bipartite settlements in the banking industry, revised quarterly on an index-slab system entirely separate from both of the above.

A PSU employee quoting an IDA figure and a central government employee quoting CDA are describing different things, even in the same month.

The 2020–21 freeze

In April 2020 the government froze DA and DR at 17%, withholding the instalments due from January 2020, July 2020 and January 2021 as a COVID-19 fiscal measure. Payment resumed from 1 July 2021 at 28%, which reflected the accumulated index movement — but the arrears for the frozen eighteen months were never paid, despite sustained representations from staff federations and pensioners' associations. It remains a live grievance and has been raised again before the 8th CPC.

DA at the pay commission changeover

At every commission changeover the accumulated DA merges into the new basic pay through the fitment factor, and DA restarts at 0%. In 2016, 125 percentage points were merged; in 2026, roughly 60 will be. This is also why HRA and other percentage-based allowances get rationalised at changeover — they would otherwise balloon on the enlarged basic. The mechanics are covered in the fitment factor guide.

DA for pensioners: Dearness Relief

Pensioners and family pensioners receive Dearness Relief (DR) at the same percentage on basic pension, notified separately by the Department of Pension & Pensioners' Welfare (doppw.gov.in). DR is paid on the full original pension even if part of it has been commuted.

Frequently asked questions

What is Dearness Allowance?

DA (महंगाई भत्ता) is inflation compensation paid as a percentage of basic pay to government employees, revised every January and July based on the AICPI-IW index. Pensioners receive the same rate as Dearness Relief (DR).

What is the DA formula?

DA % = [(12-month average of AICPI-IW (base 2016=100) − 261.42) ÷ 261.42] × 100. The Labour Bureau publishes the index monthly; the Department of Expenditure notifies the resulting rate.

Why was DA frozen in 2020-21?

The government froze three DA/DR instalments (January 2020, July 2020, January 2021) at 17% as a COVID-19 fiscal measure, resuming at 28% from July 2021. The frozen arrears were not paid.

What happens to DA under the 8th Pay Commission?

The accumulated DA (60% expected in January 2026) merges into the new basic pay via the fitment factor and the DA counter restarts from 0%.

Is DA different for bank or PSU employees?

Yes. Bank DA follows a different index slab system revised quarterly under bipartite settlements; PSU DA follows the IDA (Industrial DA) pattern. This guide covers Central Government CDA only.

Is DA paid on HRA or on gross salary?

Neither. DA is a percentage of basic pay only. The one exception is Transport Allowance, which does attract DA on itself — so ₹7,200 TPTA is worth ₹11,520 at 60% DA.

Why is DA announced months after the effective date?

The formula uses a twelve-month average of the AICPI-IW index, and the Labour Bureau publishes each month's index with a roughly two-month lag. The window for a January revision is only complete at the end of February, so the announcement typically follows in March or later, with arrears paid from the effective date.

Were the frozen 2020-21 DA instalments ever paid?

No. Three instalments — January 2020, July 2020 and January 2021 — were withheld as a COVID-19 measure and the arrears were never released, though staff federations continue to press for them.

Is bank or PSU DA the same as central government DA?

No. PSUs use Industrial DA, revised quarterly on a different base, and banks follow a separate slab system under bipartite settlements. The percentages are not comparable with central government DA.

Does DA affect my NPS or gratuity?

Yes. NPS contributions are 10% from you and 14% from the government on Basic + DA, so every DA rise increases your corpus. Gratuity is also computed on Basic + DA, so the rate on your last working day matters.
