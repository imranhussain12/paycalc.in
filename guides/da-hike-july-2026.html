DA Hike Confirmed: 63% for Central Government Employees

Updated 29 October 2026 Confirmed · Cabinet approved 28 October 2026 · effective 1 July 2026

Confirmed: the Union Cabinet approved a 3 percentage point rise on 28 October 2026, taking Dearness Allowance from 60% to 63% with effect from 1 July 2026. Because it is backdated, you also receive four months of arrears — July, August, September and October.

Every January and July, Dearness Allowance is revised to compensate Central Government employees for inflation. The instalment effective 1 July 2026 has now been approved: DA rises from 60% to 63%, and Dearness Relief for pensioners rises by the same amount.

The decision covers roughly 48.5 lakh employees and 68.7 lakh pensioners, at an annual cost to the exchequer of about ₹10,016 crore. This page sets out exactly what it is worth at your pay level, how much arrears to expect, and when it reaches your account.

How the 63% was arrived at

DA is not set by negotiation. It follows a formula tied to the All-India Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (AICPI-IW), published monthly by the Labour Bureau:

DA % = [(12-month average of AICPI-IW − 261.42) ÷ 261.42] × 100

The 261.42 baseline is the index value on 1 January 2016, the date the 7th CPC reset DA to zero. For a revision effective 1 July 2026, the relevant window runs from July 2025 to June 2026. With the June 2026 index published at 151.9 on the 2016=100 series, the twelve-month average put the computed figure at 63% after rounding — which is exactly what the Cabinet approved.

Because the formula is mechanical, the rate itself was predictable once the final month's index was published. What was not predictable was the timing: the Cabinet decision came on 28 October, later than the September date the recent pattern suggested.

Two knock-on effects are easy to miss. NPS contributions rise, because both your 10% and the government's 14% are calculated on Basic + DA — so a higher DA quietly increases your retirement corpus. And Transport Allowance earns DA on itself, so the TPTA line on your payslip also goes up.

When it reaches your account, and your arrears

The Cabinet decision came on 28 October 2026, and the Finance Ministry has issued the order implementing it. Because the rate takes effect from 1 July 2026, you have been underpaid for four months — July, August, September and October — and that difference is owed to you as arrears.

Arrears are normally released with the next convenient payroll run. Exactly when depends on your Pay & Accounts Office, so a department that has already processed November salary may push it to December.

Pensioners get the same rise

Dearness Relief moves in lockstep with DA, at the same percentage and from the same effective date. A pensioner on a basic pension of ₹30,000 currently receives ₹18,000 as DR at 60%; at 63% that becomes ₹18,900, a gain of ₹900 a month.

One detail pensioners often ask about: DR is paid on the full original pension, not the reduced amount after commutation. If you commuted 40% of your pension, your DR is still calculated on 100% of it.

How this interacts with the 8th Pay Commission

These are separate tracks that meet at the changeover. DA continues to be revised under the 7th CPC structure until the 8th CPC award is actually notified — which, on current expectations, means 2027.

At that point the accumulated DA is absorbed into the new basic pay through the fitment factor, and the DA counter restarts at zero. That is not a pay cut: it is the mechanism by which the fitment factor is larger than 1. A useful consequence is that every DA instalment between now and the changeover slightly reduces the eventual arrears, because it narrows the gap between what you are paid and what you will be paid.

There is a separate question that causes confusion. Proposals to merge DA into basic pay as an interim measure — before the commission reports — have not been accepted. That is a different thing from the absorption that happens automatically at changeover, which is standard practice and still expected. See the 8th Pay Commission guide for the full mechanism.

Frequently asked questions

How much is the July 2026 DA hike?

Three percentage points. The Union Cabinet approved the rise on 28 October 2026, taking Dearness Allowance from 60% to 63% with effect from 1 July 2026. That is 3% of your basic pay every month, plus four months of arrears for July to October.

When will the DA arrears be paid?

Arrears are normally released with the next convenient payroll run after the order is issued. The Cabinet approved on 28 October and the Finance Ministry has issued the implementing order, so most departments will pay with November or December salary. The exact month depends on your Pay & Accounts Office.

How is the DA percentage calculated?

DA % = [(12-month average of AICPI-IW − 261.42) ÷ 261.42] × 100. For a 1 July revision the window is July of the previous year to June of the current year. The Labour Bureau publishes each month's index about two months in arrears, which is why the window only closes shortly before the announcement.

Will pensioners get the same increase?

Yes. Dearness Relief for pensioners and family pensioners has been revised by the same 3 points from the same date, so DR is also 63% from 1 July 2026. Roughly 68.7 lakh pensioners are covered.

How much extra will I actually receive?

Three per cent of your basic pay per month. On a basic of ₹35,400 that is ₹1,062 a month and ₹4,248 in arrears; on ₹56,100 it is ₹1,683 a month and ₹6,732 in arrears for the four backdated months.

Does the 8th Pay Commission change this?

Not immediately. DA continues under the 7th CPC structure until the 8th CPC award is notified. At that changeover the accumulated DA is absorbed into the new basic pay through the fitment factor and the DA counter restarts at zero.
