DA Hike July 2026: 63% Projected for Central Government Employees

Updated 19 August 2026 Projection from published AICPI-IW data · Not yet notified

Short answer: the July 2026 instalment is projected at 63%, a rise of 3 percentage points from the current 60%. It is not yet approved — expect the Cabinet decision around September 2026, with arrears backdated to 1 July 2026.

Every January and July, Dearness Allowance is revised to compensate Central Government employees for inflation. The instalment effective 1 July 2026 is due now, but has not been notified. Until it is, payslips continue to show 60%.

This page explains what the published index data implies, when the announcement is likely, and exactly what it means in rupees for your pay level. Every figure here is derived from public AICPI-IW releases and clearly marked as a projection.

Why the projection is 63%

DA is not set by negotiation. It follows a formula tied to the All-India Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (AICPI-IW), published monthly by the Labour Bureau:

DA % = [(12-month average of AICPI-IW − 261.42) ÷ 261.42] × 100

The 261.42 baseline is the index value on 1 January 2016, the date the 7th CPC reset DA to zero. For a revision effective 1 July 2026, the relevant window runs from July 2025 to June 2026. With the June 2026 index published at 151.9 on the 2016=100 series, the twelve-month average puts the computed figure at roughly 63% after rounding.

Because the formula is mechanical, the outcome is fairly predictable once the final month's index is out. What is not predictable is the timing of Cabinet approval.

Two knock-on effects are easy to miss. NPS contributions rise, because both your 10% and the government's 14% are calculated on Basic + DA — so a higher DA quietly increases your retirement corpus. And Transport Allowance earns DA on itself, so the TPTA line on your payslip also goes up.

When the announcement is likely

The pattern is consistent. The Labour Bureau publishes each month's index about two months in arrears, so the twelve-month window for a July revision only closes in late August. The Cabinet then considers it, and the notification follows.

On that pattern, a September announcement would carry roughly three months of arrears — July, August and September — paid alongside a regular salary.

Pensioners get the same rise

Dearness Relief moves in lockstep with DA, at the same percentage and from the same effective date. A pensioner on a basic pension of ₹30,000 currently receives ₹18,000 as DR at 60%; at 63% that becomes ₹18,900, a gain of ₹900 a month.

One detail pensioners often ask about: DR is paid on the full original pension, not the reduced amount after commutation. If you commuted 40% of your pension, your DR is still calculated on 100% of it.

How this interacts with the 8th Pay Commission

These are separate tracks that meet at the changeover. DA continues to be revised under the 7th CPC structure until the 8th CPC award is actually notified — which, on current expectations, means 2027.

At that point the accumulated DA is absorbed into the new basic pay through the fitment factor, and the DA counter restarts at zero. That is not a pay cut: it is the mechanism by which the fitment factor is larger than 1. A useful consequence is that every DA instalment between now and the changeover slightly reduces the eventual arrears, because it narrows the gap between what you are paid and what you will be paid.

There is a separate question that causes confusion. Proposals to merge DA into basic pay as an interim measure — before the commission reports — have not been accepted. That is a different thing from the absorption that happens automatically at changeover, which is standard practice and still expected. See the 8th Pay Commission guide for the full mechanism.

Frequently asked questions

How much DA hike is expected from July 2026?

Based on the AICPI-IW index for June 2026, which stood at 151.9, the July 2026 instalment works out to a 3 percentage point rise — taking Dearness Allowance from 60% to 63%. This is a projection from published index data, not an official figure. The Union Cabinet has not yet approved it.

When will the July 2026 DA be announced?

Announcements typically follow two to three months after the effective date, so expect the Cabinet decision around September 2026. Employees continue drawing 60% until then and receive the difference as arrears back to 1 July 2026.

How is the DA percentage calculated?

DA % = [(12-month average of AICPI-IW − 261.42) ÷ 261.42] × 100. For a 1 July revision the window is July of the previous year to June of the current year. The Labour Bureau publishes each month's index about two months in arrears, which is why the window only closes shortly before the announcement.

Will pensioners get the same increase?

Yes. Dearness Relief for pensioners and family pensioners is always revised by the same percentage from the same effective date, so DR would also move from 60% to 63%.

How much extra will I actually receive?

Three per cent of your basic pay per month. On a basic of ₹35,400 that is ₹1,062 a month; on ₹56,100 it is ₹1,683. Because it is backdated to 1 July, a September announcement would bring roughly three months of arrears with it.

Does the 8th Pay Commission change this?

Not immediately. DA continues under the 7th CPC structure until the 8th CPC award is notified. At that changeover the accumulated DA is absorbed into the new basic pay through the fitment factor and the DA counter restarts at zero.

This is a projection, not an announcement. The 63% figure is derived from published AICPI-IW data using the standard formula. Only the Department of Expenditure's office memorandum makes a rate payable. Verify with your Pay & Accounts Office before relying on it.
