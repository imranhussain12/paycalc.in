Minimum pay (Level 1) is ₹18,000 under the 7th CPC; maximum (Level 18, Cabinet Secretary) is a fixed ₹2,50,000. The compression ratio between them — about 1:13.9 — has been broadly stable across commissions.

How the pay matrix replaced pay bands

Before 2016, central government pay was expressed as a pay band plus a grade pay — for example PB-2 (₹9,300–34,800) with Grade Pay 4,200. Two employees could share a pay band but sit far apart because of grade pay, and the same grade pay could appear in two different bands, which is the origin of the Grade Pay 5400 anomaly that still separates Levels 9 and 10.

The 7th CPC replaced all of it with a single table. Levels run across the top (1 to 18, plus 13A), and each column contains up to 40 vertical stages representing years of service. Your pay is now a single number located by a level and a stage. Nothing else is needed to describe it.

Three practical consequences follow. Pay fixation on promotion became arithmetic rather than interpretation. Comparing two employees became trivial. And because every cell is an explicit figure, a pay commission can revise the entire structure simply by multiplying every cell by a fitment factor — which is exactly what the 8th CPC is expected to do.

Increments and how you move down a column

Movement within a level happens through the annual increment: 3% of current basic pay, rounded up to the next multiple of ₹100. It is granted on 1 January or 1 July, depending on when you joined or were last promoted — the 7th CPC introduced the second date, replacing the single July date that had penalised employees appointed just after the cut-off.

Worked example at Level 6, entry ₹35,400: 3% is ₹1,062, rounded up to ₹1,100, giving ₹36,500 at stage 2. The next increment is 3% of ₹36,500, and so on. Because each increment is calculated on the new figure, progression compounds — which is why the top of a column is roughly three times its entry value despite a "3% a year" rule.

To qualify you need six months of service at the level before the increment date. An employee promoted in, say, March draws their next increment the following January.

Pay fixation on promotion

When you are promoted, pay is fixed under FR 22(I)(a)(1) in two steps. First you receive one notional increment in your existing level. Then you move to the new level and are placed at the first cell equal to or higher than that figure.

Example: a Level 6 employee at ₹40,100 promoted to Level 7. One notional increment gives ₹41,300. In the Level 7 column, the first cell at or above ₹41,300 is ₹44,900 — so the promoted pay is ₹44,900. Note that the gain came from the level change, not the increment; this is why promotions between adjacent levels sometimes yield less than employees expect, and why the Level 12 to 13 jump is so prized.

The same mechanism applies to MACP upgradations. Three financial upgradations are granted at 10, 20 and 30 years of service where no promotion has occurred, each moving you to the next level in the matrix.

What the 8th CPC matrix will look like

The structure is expected to survive intact — it is widely regarded as the 7th CPC's most successful reform. What changes is the value in every cell, multiplied by the fitment factor and rounded.

Watch for three things when the report is published: whether the number of levels changes (associations have asked for some to be merged), whether the 3% increment rate is revised, and whether the Level 13 anomaly correction is retained. The 7th CPC applied 2.67 rather than 2.57 at Level 13 to fix a Grade Pay 8700 distortion, which is why the ₹78,800 to ₹1,23,100 step is the largest in the matrix.

How to read your own cell

  1. Find your level on your payslip (or from your grade pay pre-2016: GP 1800 → Level 1 … GP 5400 → Level 9/10, etc.).
  2. Count your stage — each year of service at the level moves you one cell down (3% per step).
  3. Multiply your current cell value by a fitment scenario — or let the 8th CPC calculator do it with DA, HRA and NPS included.

Frequently asked questions

What is the pay matrix?

A single table introduced by the 7th CPC: pay levels 1–18 run across the top, and each column lists up to 40 stages of annual 3% increments. Your pay is defined by a level and a cell, replacing the old pay band + grade pay system.

How do increments work in the matrix?

One vertical step down your level’s column each year (3% rounded to the nearest 100), granted on 1 January or 1 July depending on when you joined or were promoted.

What happens to the matrix under the 8th CPC?

The structure is expected to survive with each cell multiplied by the new fitment factor. Some cadre-specific anomalies (Level 13 in particular, which used a 2.67 multiple in the 7th CPC) may be smoothed.

What is Level 13A?

A level inserted for Deputy Inspectors General and equivalent posts, with entry pay ₹1,31,100.

How do I find my level from my old grade pay?

Grade Pay 1800 maps to Level 1, 1900 to Level 2, 2000 to Level 3, 2400 to Level 4, 2800 to Level 5, 4200 to Level 6, 4600 to Level 7, 4800 to Level 8, 5400 in PB-2 to Level 9, 5400 in PB-3 to Level 10, 6600 to Level 11, 7600 to Level 12, 8700 to Level 13, 8900 to Level 13A and 10000 to Level 14.

How is the annual increment calculated?

Three per cent of your current basic pay, rounded up to the next multiple of ₹100, granted on 1 January or 1 July depending on your date of appointment or last promotion. You need six months of service at the level before the increment date to qualify.

How is pay fixed when I am promoted?

You are given one notional increment in your existing level, then placed at the first cell in the new level that is equal to or higher than that figure. The benefit comes mainly from the level change rather than the increment.

Will the pay matrix structure change under the 8th CPC?

The structure is expected to be retained, with each cell multiplied by the new fitment factor. What may change is the number of levels, the increment percentage, and cadre-specific anomaly corrections.
