HRA rates and the DA link

The 7th CPC set HRA at 24%, 16% and 8% of basic pay for X, Y and Z cities, with an automatic escalator: rates rose to 27/18/9 when DA crossed 25% (July 2021) and were to reach 30/20/10 when DA crossed 50% (which happened in January 2024 — the restored 30/20/10 rates apply per DoE orders; this calculator uses the widely applied 27/18/9 unless your department has notified the higher slab) (DoE orders). Under the 8th CPC, rates are expected to be rationalised again on the larger revised basic pay.

Working out which class your city is in

Only eight cities are X class: Delhi and the notified NCR areas, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad. Around ninety cities are Y class, including most state capitals — Lucknow, Jaipur, Patna, Bhopal, Chandigarh, Guwahati, Bhubaneswar, Indore, Nagpur, Coimbatore, Kochi, Visakhapatnam, Ranchi, Raipur, Dehradun and Jammu among them. Everything else is Z class.

Entitlement follows your place of posting, not where your family lives. An officer posted to a district town draws Z class HRA even if the family remains in Delhi. Classification is revised after each census, so a city can move up a class and employees there start drawing the higher rate from the notified date.

Minimum HRA and government quarters

HRA cannot fall below ₹5,400 in X cities, ₹3,600 in Y and ₹1,800 in Z. These floors derive from 30%, 20% and 10% of the ₹18,000 minimum pay, and in practice bind only for employees at the bottom of Level 1 — the calculator applies them automatically.

If you are allotted government accommodation you draw no HRA at all and pay a licence fee instead. Where both spouses are in government service and share allotted accommodation, only one entitlement applies. Full rules are in the HRA rules guide.

HRA and your tax

Under the old tax regime, Section 10(13A) exempts the least of three amounts: the HRA actually received, rent paid minus 10% of salary, and 50% of salary in metro cities or 40% elsewhere. Under the new regime — the default from FY 2026-27 — HRA is fully taxable. Employees paying substantial rent in metros are often the one group for whom the old regime still wins, so it is worth comparing both in the income tax calculator before you choose.

Frequently asked questions

What are the current HRA rates for Central Government employees?

X class (metro) cities: 27% of basic pay. Y class cities: 18%. Z class (all other) cities: 9%. These rates apply after DA crossed 50%, per the 7th CPC formula.

Which cities are X class for HRA?

Eight cities with population above 50 lakh: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad.

Is there a minimum HRA?

Yes. HRA cannot be less than ₹5,400 (X), ₹3,600 (Y) or ₹1,800 (Z) per month — floors set at 30%, 20% and 10% of the ₹18,000 minimum pay.

Is HRA taxable for government employees?

HRA is exempt under Section 10(13A) to the extent of the least of: actual HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of salary, and 50% (metro) or 40% (non-metro) of salary — but only in the old tax regime. In the new regime HRA is fully taxable.

Does my HRA change if I am transferred?

Yes. HRA follows your place of posting from the date you join the new station, so a transfer between city classes changes your entitlement immediately.

Why is my HRA showing the minimum amount?

Because the percentage of your basic pay falls below the floor for your city class — ₹5,400 in X, ₹3,600 in Y, ₹1,800 in Z. The calculator applies whichever is higher.

Will HRA percentages change under the 8th CPC?

Probably. The 7th CPC cut them from 30/20/10 to 24/16/8 because the new basic pay was 2.57 times larger, then restored them in steps as DA crossed 25% and 50%. Expect a similar pattern — though the rupee amount still rises because the base multiplies.
