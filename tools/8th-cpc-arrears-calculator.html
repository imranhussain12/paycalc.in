How this estimate works

Every pay commission in recent memory has been implemented after its effective date, with the gap settled as arrears. The 6th CPC (effective January 2006, implemented September 2008) produced roughly 32 months of arrears paid in two instalments; the 7th CPC (effective January 2016, implemented mid-2016) produced six months. For the 8th CPC, most projections put the report and Cabinet approval in 2027 — meaning 12 to 24 months of arrears backdated to 1 January 2026.

The estimate here is deliberately conservative and simple: it compares your projected new basic pay against your current basic + DA, because the fitment factor absorbs DA at the changeover. It does not model the DA instalments you may receive between January 2026 and implementation (each of which slightly reduces the true difference), nor annual increments falling in the arrears window.

Two lessons. Arrears periods vary enormously — the 6th CPC's 32 months against the 7th's six. And payment is not guaranteed to be a single lump sum: the 6th CPC deliberately staggered it across financial years to spread the fiscal impact, which also spread the tax burden.

Note that at the 7th CPC, allowances were not paid as arrears at all. Revised allowances took effect only from 1 July 2017, eighteen months after pay. Plan for arrears on basic pay, not on HRA or transport allowance.

Section 89(1) relief, and why it matters here

Arrears are taxable in the year you receive them, not the years they relate to. A single large payment can therefore push you into a higher slab for that one year — an artificial outcome the law provides a remedy for.

Section 89(1) lets you recompute tax as though the arrears had been received in the years they were due, and claim the difference as relief. The process: work out tax for the year of receipt with and without the arrears, then recompute the earlier years as if the arrears had been included, and claim the difference.

The procedural trap is simple and catches many people. You must file Form 10E on the income tax e-filing portal before submitting your return. Claiming relief without filing Form 10E means the claim is disallowed and you receive a notice. It takes ten minutes online and can save tens of thousands of rupees on a large arrears payment.

Planning for the payment

Arrears are a windfall in timing but not in substance — they are salary you were owed. A few things are worth thinking about before it lands. NPS subscribers will see contributions deducted on the arrears too, increasing the corpus. If you have a floating-rate loan, a lump sum prepayment saves more interest than most alternative uses. And because the amount is taxable, setting aside the tax portion when it arrives avoids an unpleasant surprise the following July.

Frequently asked questions

How are 8th CPC arrears calculated?

Arrears equal the monthly difference between your revised pay (basic × fitment factor) and what you actually drew (basic + DA), multiplied by the number of months from 1 January 2026 to the implementation month.

Are pay commission arrears taxable?

Yes, arrears are taxable as salary in the year you receive them. Section 89(1) of the Income-tax Act provides relief by spreading the income back to the years it relates to — claim it by filing Form 10E before your return.

How many months of arrears did 7th CPC employees get?

Six months. The 7th CPC was effective 1 January 2016 and revised salaries were paid from the July–August 2016 payroll, so arrears covered January to June 2016.

Will arrears include allowances?

Historically, no. The 7th CPC paid arrears on pay and pension but revised allowances (like HRA) only prospectively from 1 July 2017. Plan for arrears on basic pay, not allowances.

Will 8th CPC arrears be paid in one instalment?

Unknown. The 7th CPC paid six months of arrears in a single payment, but the 6th CPC staggered roughly 32 months across two financial years as 40% and 60%. A longer arrears period makes staggering more likely.

Do arrears include HRA and transport allowance?

Historically no. At the 7th CPC, revised allowances took effect only from 1 July 2017, eighteen months after pay. Plan for arrears on basic pay alone.

What happens if I claim Section 89(1) relief without Form 10E?

The relief is disallowed and you will typically receive a notice from the income tax department. Form 10E must be filed on the e-filing portal before you submit your return.
