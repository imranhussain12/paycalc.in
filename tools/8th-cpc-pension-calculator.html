How pension revision works at a pay commission changeover

Central Government pension is 50% of the last basic pay drawn (or average emoluments of the last 10 months, whichever is higher), under the CCS (Pension) Rules, 2021. When a new pay commission takes effect, existing pensioners' basic pension is revised so that older retirees are not left behind newer ones — the principle of parity that the 7th CPC strengthened.

At the 7th CPC changeover, pensions were revised by the higher of two options: (1) multiply the existing basic pension by the fitment factor 2.57, or (2) re-fix notionally in the new pay matrix based on the level and stage at retirement. The 8th CPC is expected to follow a similar approach, which is why this calculator applies your chosen fitment factor directly to your current basic pension (Department of Pension & Pensioners' Welfare).

Commutation, briefly

You may commute (exchange) up to 40% of your basic pension for an immediate lump sum. The lump sum is calculated as: commuted monthly amount × 12 × commutation factor (8.194 if your next birthday is your 60th). Your monthly pension is reduced by the commuted amount for 15 years from the date of commutation, after which it is fully restored. Dearness Relief is always paid on the full revised pension, not the reduced one.

Commutation in practice

Commutation lets you exchange up to 40% of your basic pension for an immediate lump sum. The amount is your commuted monthly pension × 12 × a commutation factor from the CCS Commutation Table, which depends on your age at your next birthday — 8.194 at 60, higher at younger ages and lower at older ones.

Two details are frequently misunderstood. First, the reduction lasts 15 years from the date of commutation, after which the full pension is automatically restored — no application is needed, though it is worth checking your pension slip in the fifteenth year. Second, Dearness Relief is always paid on the full original pension, not the reduced one, which materially softens the cost of commuting.

Whether to commute is a genuine financial judgement rather than an obvious choice. The lump sum is tax-free and useful for clearing a home loan or funding a child's education or marriage. Against that, you surrender fifteen years of a portion of an inflation-indexed, guaranteed income — the most valuable kind. Commuting to invest in market instruments rarely makes sense once you account for the DR you keep either way.

Family pension

Family pension is normally 30% of the last pay drawn, subject to a minimum of ₹9,000 a month under the 7th CPC. An enhanced rate of 50% applies for the first ten years after death in service, or for seven years after retirement or until the deceased would have turned 67, whichever is earlier.

It passes to the spouse first, then to eligible children — sons until 25 or employment, unmarried daughters without a time limit, and disabled children for life. Since 2021 the rules also cover divorced daughters and, in specified circumstances, dependent parents and siblings with disabilities.

Additional old-age pension applies on top: 20% extra from age 80, 30% from 85, 40% from 90, 50% from 95 and 100% from 100. This applies to family pensioners as well as to the pensioner.

How the 8th CPC will revise your pension

At the 7th CPC, existing pensioners received whichever of two options was higher: their basic pension multiplied by 2.57, or a notional re-fixation in the new pay matrix based on the level and stage from which they retired. The second option addressed pension parity — an officer who retired in 1990 was otherwise left far behind one who retired from the same post in 2015.

Expect the same two-option approach from the 8th CPC. Dearness Relief will reset to 0% on the revised pension and rebuild from there, exactly as DA does for serving employees. This calculator applies the simpler multiplication method, which is the more conservative of the two.

Frequently asked questions

How will pension be revised under the 8th Pay Commission?

Existing basic pension is expected to be multiplied by the fitment factor recommended by the commission, with Dearness Relief (DR) resetting to 0% — the same method used at the 7th CPC changeover in 2016.

What is commutation of pension?

A pensioner may exchange up to 40% of basic pension for a lump sum. The monthly pension reduces by the commuted percentage for 15 years, after which the full pension is restored. The lump sum equals commuted amount × 12 × the commutation factor from the CCS Commutation Table (8.194 at age 60).

How is family pension calculated?

Family pension is normally 30% of the last pay drawn, subject to a minimum (₹9,000 per month under the 7th CPC). Enhanced family pension of 50% applies for the first 7 years after death in service or until the deceased would have turned 67.

Will Dearness Relief continue after the 8th CPC?

Yes. DR mirrors DA and is revised every January and July. After the merger at changeover it restarts from 0% on the revised pension.

Is the minimum pension also revised?

Yes. The 7th CPC minimum pension is ₹9,000 per month. At a fitment factor of 2.57 it would become about ₹23,130; at 2.86 about ₹25,740.

Is Dearness Relief paid on the reduced pension after commutation?

No — DR is always calculated on your full original basic pension, not the amount reduced by commutation. This significantly reduces the effective cost of commuting.

When is my commuted pension restored?

Automatically after 15 years from the date of commutation. No application is required, but it is worth checking your pension slip in the fifteenth year to confirm the restoration was made.

What is the enhanced family pension rate?

Fifty per cent of last pay for the first ten years after death in service, or seven years after retirement or until the deceased would have turned 67, whichever is earlier. The normal rate is 30%, with a minimum of ₹9,000 per month.

Do pensioners get additional pension in old age?

Yes — 20% extra from age 80, 30% from 85, 40% from 90, 50% from 95 and 100% from age 100. This applies to family pensioners too.
