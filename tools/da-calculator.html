How DA is set

DA compensates government employees for inflation. It is computed from the All-India Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (AICPI-IW), published monthly by the Labour Bureau (labourbureau.gov.in). The Department of Expenditure notifies the revised rate twice a year — effective 1 January and 1 July — usually announced two to three months later, with the difference paid as arrears (DoE orders).

Pensioners receive the same percentage as Dearness Relief (DR) on basic pension.

What DA is paid on

DA is a percentage of basic pay only — not gross salary, and not HRA. The one exception worth knowing is Transport Allowance, which attracts DA on itself. A Level 9 employee drawing ₹7,200 TPTA actually receives ₹11,520 once 60% DA is applied. This single rule accounts for most payslip reconciliation confusion.

DA also drives several other figures. Your NPS contribution is 10% of Basic + DA, with the government adding 14% on the same base, so every DA rise quietly increases your retirement corpus. Gratuity on retirement is likewise computed on Basic + DA, which means the DA rate on your final working day has a permanent effect on the lump sum.

Why announcements lag the effective date

The formula averages the AICPI-IW index over twelve months, and the Labour Bureau publishes each month's index roughly two months in arrears. The window for a 1 January revision is therefore only complete at the end of February, so the Cabinet decision typically lands in March, and for a 1 July revision around September or October. Employees continue on the old rate in the meantime and receive the difference as arrears — which is why a DA "hike" often arrives as a lump sum alongside a festival month.

The 1 July 2026 instalment is currently in exactly this position: due, but not yet notified.

Do not compare CDA with IDA or bank DA

Central government employees are on CDA, revised half-yearly. Central PSU employees are on Industrial DA, revised quarterly on a different base. Bank employees follow a slab system under bipartite settlements, also quarterly. The percentages are not comparable across these systems even in the same month — a PSU colleague quoting a very different number is not wrong, just on a different scheme. The DA guide works through the formula in detail.

Frequently asked questions

What is the current DA rate for Central Government employees?

The Dearness Allowance rate is 60% of basic pay, effective 1 January 2026. DA is revised twice a year, in January and July.

How is DA calculated?

DA % = [(average AICPI-IW for the past 12 months − 261.42) ÷ 261.42] × 100, using the 2016=100 index series. The result is rounded and notified by the Department of Expenditure.

When is the next DA revision?

The 1 July 2026 instalment is due but has not been notified yet. Announcements usually follow two to three months after the effective date, so expect it around September–October 2026, paid with arrears back to 1 July. Until then employees continue to draw 60%. If the 8th CPC is implemented with effect from January 2026, DA instead resets to 0% on the revised basic pay.

Is DA taxable?

Yes, DA is fully taxable as part of salary income for serving employees.

Is DA calculated on gross salary?

No — on basic pay only. It is not paid on HRA. The single exception is Transport Allowance, which does attract DA on itself.

Does a DA increase affect my NPS and gratuity?

Yes. NPS contributions are 10% from you and 14% from the government on Basic + DA, and gratuity is computed on Basic + DA, so a DA rise increases both.

Has the July 2026 DA been announced?

Not yet. The instalment is due with effect from 1 July 2026 but has not been notified. Announcements typically follow two to three months after the effective date, so expect it around September–October 2026, paid with arrears back to 1 July.
